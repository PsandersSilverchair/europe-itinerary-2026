The Eiffel Tower

Sun Jun 21 • 5:30 PM • 2nd floor by lift
The Big Picture The tower belongs to Paris's "iron age" of confidence — and to the trip's theme of buildings that changed jobs. It was a "temporary" fair exhibit that became an eternal icon. Built for the 1889 World's Fair marking the 100th anniversary of the French Revolution, it was the tallest structure on Earth for 40 years.

📖Before You Go — The Story

Gustave Eiffel's company built the tower in just 2 years, 2 months and 5 days (1887–1889) out of about 18,000 individually calculated iron pieces. At 330 m it was the world's tallest structure until New York's Chrysler Building topped it in 1929.

Here's the great lesson hiding in it: people hated it. In 1887, some 300 famous artists and writers signed a furious protest calling it a "useless and monstrous" eyesore. And it was only ever meant to stand 20 years — slated for demolition in 1909. What saved it? Radio. The antenna on top made it too useful for telegraphy to tear down.

You're booked for the 2nd floor, and that's not a consolation prize — many guides argue it has the better view: high enough for the grand sweep, low enough to still pick out the city's details (the summit is higher but often hazier).

It's repainted by hand — by climbers with brushes — roughly every seven years.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • People hated it! 300 famous Parisians signed a petition against it before it was even finished.
  • It was supposed to be scrap metal by 1909 — radio saved it.
  • Count how long Eiffel took to build it: just 2 years and 2 months.
  • It's painted by hand, by climbers with brushes, every ~7 years.
  • Myth-buster: family photos of the tower at night are totally fine — the "it's illegal" line only concerns commercial use.
