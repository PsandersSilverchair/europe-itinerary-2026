Lauterbrunnen Valley & Trümmelbach Falls

Fri Jun 26 • Last full day in the Alps
The Big Picture This is "ice as architect" you can stand inside. Ice-age glaciers ground Lauterbrunnen into a textbook U-shaped trough — sheer walls, flat floor — and left side-streams hanging in mid-air, which is why the valley has its famous ~72 waterfalls. It's also "the valley that launched a thousand stories": Goethe, Byron, and very probably Tolkien were all moved by this exact view.

📖Before You Go — The Story

Staubbach Falls free-falls 297 m off a hanging valley — its name means "dust" for the way the water drifts into spray. Goethe wrote a poem here in 1779; Byron, in 1816, described it as "like the tail of a white horse streaming in the wind," and the scenery fed his drama Manfred.

The showstopper is Trümmelbach Falls: ten glacier-fed waterfalls roaring inside the mountain, reached by a tunnel lift and galleries. It drains the Eiger–Mönch–Jungfrau snows at up to 20,000 litres per second — and over thousands of years the gritty glacier water drilled smooth corkscrew chutes through solid rock.

And for fantasy fans: a 19-year-old J.R.R. Tolkien hiked through here in 1911. He later said the hobbit's journey "from Rivendell to the other side of the Misty Mountains" was based on that 1911 adventure, and his own painting of Rivendell looks strikingly like this valley. He never quite said "Lauterbrunnen is Rivendell" — but it's almost certainly his inspiration.

🧭On Tour — Easy Logistics

Morning
Cog rail down to Lauterbrunnen
~15 min from Wengen. Staubbach is a 10-minute walk from the station — best in morning light.
Midday
Trümmelbach Falls
~3 km down-valley (Stechelberg-bound PostBus or a flat walk/bike). Ride the lift into the mountain.
Afternoon
Valley picnic, then home
Optional add-on if energy holds: the Schilthorn/Piz Gloria Bond cable car from Stechelberg.
Evening
Farewell Swiss dinner
Raclette or rösti back in Wengen — celebrate an amazing trip.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • You might be walking through the real Rivendell — bring a Hobbit map and compare!
  • Trümmelbach is a waterfall you ride an elevator into — 20,000 litres a second thundering past.
  • Waterfall challenge: can you count 10 of the valley's 72 falls?
  • The corkscrew rock chutes were drilled by glacier sand — nature's own waterslide (no riding!).

"72 waterfalls" is a beloved round number, not a survey — the count rises and falls with rain and snowmelt. Late June after the melt is prime.
