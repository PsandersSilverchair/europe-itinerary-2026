Tower of London

Tue Jun 16 • 10:00 AM • Beefeater tour
The Big Picture When the Normans conquered England in 1066, they built in stone to intimidate. The White Tower (begun ~1078, in pale stone shipped from Caen in France) was designed to loom over Londoners. Pair it in your mind with Westminster Abbey: the Tower is raw power (prisoners and treasure), the Abbey is sacred legitimacy. The Crown Jewels have lived here since 1661.

📖Before You Go — The Story

The Tower is really a small walled village that's been a royal palace, fortress, mint, armoury, prison, and even a royal zoo (lions and a polar bear once lived here) across more than 950 years.

Your guides are the Yeoman Warders, nicknamed "Beefeaters." Every one is a retired senior member of the armed forces with at least 22 years' service — and they live inside the fortress with their families. Their tour is funny, dramatic, and included with your ticket.

The grim star is Tower Green, the "private" execution spot for VIPs — including two of Henry VIII's queens (Anne Boleyn, beheaded 1536 by a swordsman brought from France, and Catherine Howard) and 16-year-old Lady Jane Grey, queen for just nine days. They lie in the little chapel beside the green.

Then there are the famous ravens. Legend says if they ever leave, the Crown and Britain will fall — so at least six are kept, with names and a keeper called the Ravenmaster. Fun twist: the Tower's own historian thinks the legend is "likely a Victorian flight of fantasy," with no hard evidence of ravens here before 1883. Tell both versions!

And the Crown Jewels: over 100 objects set with more than 23,000 gemstones, viewed from a moving walkway so the crowd keeps flowing.
