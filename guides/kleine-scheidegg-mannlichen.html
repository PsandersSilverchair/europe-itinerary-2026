Kleine Scheidegg & the Männlichen Royal Walk

A flexible Wengen day
The Big Picture This is the region's mountaineering drama up close. Kleine Scheidegg is the natural grandstand for the Eiger's north face — the "murder wall" that killed climbers through the 1930s before it was finally conquered in 1938. And the family-friendly Männlichen "Royal Walk" literally ends at a crown — perfect for your royal-history crew.

📖Before You Go — The Story

The cog train you ride is the Wengernalp Railway, opened in 1893 — at ~19.1 km, the world's longest continuous rack-and-pinion railway. It climbs to Kleine Scheidegg (2,061 m), the pass where you change trains for the Jungfraujoch.

From here you stare straight up the Eiger north face, a ~1,800 m wall nicknamed the Mordwand ("murder wall"). In the 1930s climbers died trying it — most famously in the 1936 disaster — while crowds watched through telescopes from the hotel terraces. It was finally climbed in July 1938 by a four-man team who teamed up mid-climb. (Calibrate this story for the kids — it has a sharp edge.)

For a gentler triumph, ride the cable car up to Männlichen (2,343 m) and do the short Royal Walk to a crown-shaped viewing platform, then stroll the easy, gently downhill Panorama Trail to Kleine Scheidegg with the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau on your left the whole way.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • Stand inside a giant crown at the Männlichen summit — monarch of the mountain!
  • Find the tiny row of windows in the Eiger's face — that's the railway tunnel hiding inside.
  • Listen for marmots whistling along the Panorama Trail.
  • The Eiger's nickname is the "murder wall" — and the 1938 team won by teaming up.
