An Evening in Le Marais

Sat Jun 20 • Evening (after Versailles)
The Big Picture The Marais is the trip's "old Paris vs. new Paris" theme you can taste. When Baron Haussmann bulldozed wide boulevards through the medieval city in the 1850s–60s, he largely missed the Marais — so its crooked, narrow streets are the surviving "before" photo of Paris. It's also the historic heart of Jewish Paris.

📖Before You Go — The Story

The area around the Rue des Rosiers is known as the Pletzl ("little place" in Yiddish), the historic heart of Jewish Paris, with roots back to the 13th century and waves of arrivals in the 1800s. It's still alive with synagogues, bakeries, and the Shoah Memorial — living history, not a theme set, even as boutiques have moved in.

A few steps away is Place des Vosges, built 1605–1612 under Henry IV and the oldest planned square in Paris. It was inaugurated with festivities for the engagement of a young Louis XIII. Writer Victor Hugo (of The Hunchback of Notre-Dame) later lived at No. 6, now a free museum.

Foodwise, the legend is L'As du Fallafel at 34 Rue des Rosiers — a falafel-eggplant-hummus pita so good the queue runs down the street. Important: it's kosher, so it closes Friday evening through Saturday for Shabbat. Your plan is Saturday evening, so check whether it has reopened after sundown — and if not, plenty of other falafel windows and bistros line the same streets.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • A falafel pita the size of your head, eaten standing in a medieval street.
  • Crooked = old: the Marais's wonky streets escaped Haussmann's bulldozers.
  • Royal bonus for Levi: Place des Vosges opened with a party for an 11-year-old king's engagement.
  • Find No. 6 — Victor Hugo's house — on the square.
