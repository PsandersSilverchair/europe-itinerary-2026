Musée d'Orsay & Bouillon Chartier

Sun Jun 21 • Before the 5:30 PM Eiffel tour
The Big Picture Two perfect examples of Paris's "buildings that changed jobs" theme. The Orsay is a grand railway station reborn as an art museum; Bouillon Chartier is a 19th-century workers' canteen that simply never changed. Both are windows into the optimistic belle époque around 1900.

📖Before You Go — The Story

A train station full of Impressionists

The Gare d'Orsay opened in 1900 for the World's Fair — the world's first electrified urban terminal station. It closed to long trains, sat empty, and reopened in 1986 as the Musée d'Orsay, now home to the world's greatest collection of Impressionist and post-Impressionist art: Monet, Renoir, Degas, Cézanne, Van Gogh, Gauguin. The giant station clocks are still there — and you can stand behind one and look out over Paris through the glass (very Hugo Cabret).

Look for Renoir's Bal du moulin de la Galette (1876) — painted up in Montmartre, the exact streets you'll walk tomorrow morning. Seeing the painting first makes the walk magical.

Dinner the way the market workers ate

Bouillon Chartier opened in 1896 to feed working Parisians fast and cheap, and it has barely changed since: waiters in black waistcoats and long aprons still scribble your bill right on the paper tablecloth. It's a classified historic monument — and dinner can cost less than a Paris souvenir.
