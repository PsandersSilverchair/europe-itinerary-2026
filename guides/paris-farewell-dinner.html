Farewell Paris Dinner

Mon Jun 22 • Evening • Les Halles
The Big Picture Your apartment sits in Les Halles — for centuries the central food market of Paris, Émile Zola's "belly of Paris." The classic bistros around you grew up to feed the market's butchers and vegetable-haulers around the clock. The market moved out in 1969–71, but the restaurants — and their onion soup — never left. A fitting last taste of "old Paris."

📖Before You Go — The Story

Two neighborhood classics, both a 5-minute walk away:

Au Pied de Cochon ("At the Pig's Foot") opened in 1947 to feed market workers and famously ran 24 hours a day for decades. Its French onion soup is the signature — they serve hundreds of bowls a day. There's plenty of chicken, steak-frites and seafood for anyone not feeling adventurous.

La Poule au Pot, founded 1935, is a barely-changed late-night bistro named after King Henry IV's famous campaign promise of "a chicken in every pot." A lovely royal-history full-circle for Levi: it's the same King Henry IV who built the Pont Neuf and Place des Vosges you've already seen this trip.

Bonus, 30 seconds from your door: the Tour Jean-sans-Peur on Rue Étienne Marcel, a genuine medieval tower surviving among the modern shops — a perfect last look at old Paris.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • The restaurant is literally called "At the Pig's Foot" — dare someone to order it!
  • These bistros once served market workers at 4 a.m. — the onion soup was their pick-me-up.
  • "A chicken in every pot" was a real royal promise — from the same king who built the Pont Neuf.
  • Find the hidden medieval tower (Tour Jean-sans-Peur) right on your street.
