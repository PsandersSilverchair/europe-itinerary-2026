The Real Mary King's Close

Thu Jun 11 • 5:00 PM (check in 4:50)
The Big Picture Remember how Edinburgh's old city grew down into its closes? This is what happened to one of them. When the Royal Exchange (today's City Chambers) was built in the 1750s, the top of Mary King's Close was knocked down and the rest was buried under it as foundations — accidentally preserving a whole 17th-century street underground. You're about to walk into the chapter of the city you walked over earlier today.

📖Before You Go — The Story

Mary King was a real person — a widowed merchant and fabric trader who lived and did business on this close in the 1630s. Back then it was a busy, vertical street of shops and homes, stacked high and packed tight.

Then, in 1645, the plague came. It spread fastest through exactly these overcrowded closes; estimates say a quarter to a third of Edinburgh's people died. The sick were quarantined — and, importantly, the city brought them food and water (1645's version of contactless delivery). Edinburgh even had a plague doctor, Dr. George Rae, who visited in head-to-toe leather with a beaked mask stuffed with herbs.

A century later the upper close was demolished and built over, sealing the lower stories away. It reopened to the public as The Real Mary King's Close in 2003. Your visit is a guided, costumed walk through real rooms where real people lived, traded, and survived.

One stop has become famous: Annie's Room. In 1992 a visiting Japanese psychic said she sensed a little girl who'd lost her doll. Visitors have left a growing pile of dolls and toys for "Annie" ever since. Touching tribute or pure nightmare fuel? You decide.

🧭On Tour — What to Expect

It's a guided tour of about an hour, led by a costumed character — atmospheric and dark, but a history tour, not a jump-scare haunted house. Perfect for jet-lagged legs: indoors, all-weather, and seated/standing at an easy pace.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Be Evidence Detectives

Lots of spooky stories down here. Your job: decide what's history and what's legend.

  • Find Annie's Room and its pile of dolls. Verdict: sweet or creepy?
  • The plague doctor's costume — a beaked leather mask packed with herbs. Why a beak?
  • Myth-buster: some people say plague victims were "bricked up alive" here. That's not true — the city fed its sick and the close was emptied before being built over. Catch anyone repeating the myth!
  • It's not a secret tunnel city — it's the lower floors of an ordinary street, buried by later building.
