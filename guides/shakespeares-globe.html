Shakespeare's Globe

Sun Jun 14 • 6:30 PM • A Midsummer Night's Dream
The Big Picture London's story is "invaders become ingredients" — the city absorbs everyone. The Globe is a perfect example: it's a faithful 1997 reconstruction championed by an American actor, Sam Wanamaker. And notice the geography theme: the original playhouse went up on the "lawless" South Bank in 1599 because the respectable City fathers banned theatres inside the walls. You're standing exactly where forbidden fun happened 400+ years ago.

📖Before You Go — The Story

The first Globe opened in 1599 and was the home of Shakespeare's company. In 1613 it burned to the ground in about an hour when a stage cannon fired during Henry VIII and set the thatched roof alight. A contemporary letter says one man's breeches caught fire and were put out with a bottle of ale — and, amazingly, nobody died.

The theatre you'll visit is a careful reconstruction that opened in 1997, about 230 m from the original site, built the old way: green oak, wooden pegs, and a real thatched roof (a rarity in central London since the Great Fire of 1666). It was the decades-long dream of American actor-director Sam Wanamaker.

You're standing in the Yard as "groundlings" — the rowdy, cheap-ticket crowd Shakespeare's actors had to win over, rain or shine, because the Yard is open to the sky. Far from being bad seats, the Yard is the most immersive spot in the house: the actors play to you.

And A Midsummer Night's Dream is the ideal first Shakespeare for kids — fairies, a love-potion mix-up, and a man whose head gets turned into a donkey's. Slapstick and magic.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • Look up: a real thatched roof — almost unheard of in central London because of the 1666 Great Fire.
  • You're groundlings, just like the crowds in 1599 — the actors will talk and play straight to you.
  • Watch for Bottom the weaver getting a donkey's head — the play's biggest laugh.
  • Fire fact: the first Globe burned down because of a cannon during a play. (This one is much more careful!)
