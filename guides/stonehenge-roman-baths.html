Stonehenge & the Roman Baths

Thu Jun 18 • 8:15 AM • Full-day coach tour
The Big Picture Today you bookend the whole region's story. Stone keeps the score: Stonehenge is the oldest "build something enormous to say who we are" on the island, and the Roman Baths show the trip's other big theme — invaders become ingredients. The Romans didn't erase the local goddess of Bath's hot spring; they merged her with their own and built a temple to "Sulis Minerva."

📖Before You Go — The Story

Stonehenge: 4,500-year-old engineering

The circular earthwork went up around 3000 BC; the giant stone settings around 2500 BC. The big "sarsen" stones average 25 tons and came from ~16 miles away (pinpointed by 2020 research); the smaller "bluestones" were hauled all the way from the Preseli Hills in Wales, over 150 miles. No wheels, no metal tools, no writing — just rope, sledges, log rollers, and hundreds of people who clearly thought it mattered.

Bath: a 2,000-year-old water park

At a hot spring the Celts already held sacred, the Romans built the temple-and-bath complex of Aquae Sulis around AD 60–70. The spring still gushes up to 46°C, about 240,000 gallons a day. Archaeologists found around 130 lead "curse tablets" thrown into the Sacred Spring — ancient rage-texts asking the goddess to punish whoever stole someone's cloak — plus 12,000+ coins tossed in as offerings.

Centuries later, Georgian Bath reinvented itself as the elegant resort of the Royal Crescent (1767–75); Jane Austen lived here 1801–06 (and privately disliked it). The whole city is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
