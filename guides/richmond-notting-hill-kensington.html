Ted Lasso's Richmond, Notting Hill & Kensington

Wed Jun 17 • Daytime
The Big Picture Today is the trip's "Americans fall for England, and England plays along" theme — and it's personal. You're from Richmond, Virginia; today you visit Richmond, London, made world-famous again by an American TV coach. Sam Wanamaker rebuilt the Globe; Ted Lasso charmed Richmond — same story, 50 years apart.

📖Before You Go — The Story

Believe: Ted Lasso's Richmond

The pub from the show — the fictional "Crown & Anchor" — is really The Prince's Head at 28 The Green, Richmond. Only the exterior is used; the interior is a studio set, so the rooms won't match the show. Ted's flat doorway is on pretty Paved Court, and Ted and Coach Beard's walks cross Richmond Green. Fun family link: both Richmonds trace back to Richmond Palace, named after Henry VII's Yorkshire earldom.

Nearby Richmond Park was walled as a royal hunting ground by Charles I in 1637 and is home to 600+ wild red and fallow deer. They're descended from royal herds — and they are wild: keep your distance (signs say 50 m) and never feed them.

Notting Hill & Portobello Road

Famous for pastel houses and the antiques market — now the UK's largest, with around 1,000 vendors, and the backdrop of the 1999 film. The street is named after Portobello Farm, itself named for a 1739 British naval victory at Porto Bello, Panama. Note: Saturday is the big antiques day; Jun 17 is a Wednesday, so expect a quieter market with shops and arcades open.

Kensington Palace

Queen Victoria was born here in 1819 and raised under the strict "Kensington System." It was later Princess Diana's home, and today it's the official London residence of the Prince and Princess of Wales. The gardens are free to wander.
