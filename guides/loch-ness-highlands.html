Loch Ness, Glen Coe & the Highlands

Sat Jun 13 • 7:45 AM (check-in closes 7:30)
The Big Picture The Highlands tell Scotland's "crowns, oaths and broken trust" story from the wild side. After the deposed King James lost his throne in 1689, Highland clans loyal to him were ordered to swear allegiance to the new king. One chief was late — and the government made a horrifying example of his people at Glen Coe. Add a glacier-carved landscape and the world's most famous monster, and you've got a full Scottish day.

📖Before You Go — The Story

Glen Coe: "murder under trust"

In 1692, soldiers were billeted as guests in the homes of the MacDonalds of Glencoe for nearly two weeks. Then, at 5 a.m. on 13 February, they turned on their hosts and killed around 38 of them. It wasn't a simple clan feud — it was a government operation, planned in Edinburgh and London and signed off at the top. Scots remember it as "murder under trust," and Glen Coe's beauty has felt haunted ever since.

Loch Ness: a very good hiding place

Loch Ness is up to ~230 m (755 ft) deep and holds more water than every lake in England and Wales combined. If a monster wanted somewhere to hide, this is the spot. The modern "Nessie" craze began in 1933. The most famous photo — the 1934 "Surgeon's Photograph" — was confessed in 1994 to be a toy submarine with a sculpted clay neck. The hoaxers kept the secret for 60 years.

The drive itself

Rabbie's runs this as a small-group day in a 16-seat mini-coach — about 12 hours. You'll pass the giant Kelpies sculptures and Stirling Castle, cross the Highland Boundary Fault, traverse wild Rannoch Moor to Glen Coe, and stop at Fort Augustus on Loch Ness (with an optional ~50-minute boat cruise).
