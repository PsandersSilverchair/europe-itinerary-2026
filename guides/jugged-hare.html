The Jugged Hare

Wed Jun 17 • 8:00 PM • Party of 4
The Big Picture Your dinner sits on a piece of brewing history. The Jugged Hare occupies a corner of the old Whitbread Brewery on Chiswell Street — founded by Samuel Whitbread in 1750 as the country's first purpose-built mass-production brewery. Beer was made on this street until 1976. It's a fitting "stone keeps the score" footnote to your London days.

📖Before You Go — The Story

The corner building was the brewery's own pub (the "King's Head," recorded by 1796) — essentially the brewery tap. Today's gastropub opened in 2012 and leans hard into a British country-house-hunt theme: think seasonal game, spit-roast meats, wild fish — and walls of taxidermy birds and hares.

So what's a "jugged hare"? It's a genuinely old English dish: hare slow-cooked in a tall jug or jar, traditionally thickened with the animal's own blood. Peak gross-out trivia to spring on the kids at the table.

A famous cooking line, "First, catch your hare," is usually pinned on cookbook writer Hannah Glasse — but it's a misquotation. She actually wrote "Take your hare when it is cased…" A nice myth to bust over dinner.

Don't worry: it's a gastropub, so alongside the venison and game there are roasts, fish and chips, and burgers. June menus lean to fish, lamb and farmed game (wild game birds are mostly an autumn thing).

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • Count the creatures: how many stuffed birds and hares can you find around the room?
  • Ask what "jugged hare" actually is. (Brace yourself for the blood part.)
  • Just outside: the Barbican's giant concrete towers and lakeside terrace — wonderfully strange.
  • This corner was a brewery for over 200 years — sniff out any old brewery signs.
