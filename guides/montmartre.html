Montmartre & Sacré-Cœur

Mon Jun 22 • Morning (Louvre at 3:30 PM)
The Big Picture Montmartre is the trip's "village vs. the city below" theme. The butte was cheap, semi-rural and dotted with windmills, so the painters moved in — Renoir, later Picasso and Van Gogh. Crowning it is Sacré-Cœur, a church whose meaning Parisians still argue about. It's the artists' Paris, looking down on the grand boulevards.

📖Before You Go — The Story

Sacré-Cœur looks ancient but isn't: it was begun in 1875 and finished in 1914 — younger than the Eiffel Tower. It was built as a "national vow" after France's crushing defeat by Prussia in 1870–71, and its link to the rebel Paris Commune (which began on Montmartre) is genuinely debated to this day. Its special travertine stone does something magical: it bleaches whiter with rain and age — the church is, in effect, self-cleaning. Up in the tower hangs the "Savoyarde," a bell weighing about 19 tonnes (roughly three elephants).

This is also where art history happened on the street. Renoir painted his famous Bal du moulin de la Galette here in 1876 (you saw it at the Orsay yesterday), working from a studio on Rue Cortot, now the Musée de Montmartre. The windmill still stands — now a restaurant.
