Seine Cruise — Bateaux Parisiens

Fri Jun 19 • Arrival afternoon
The Big Picture Every Paris stop is really one story told eight ways — and it begins with the river. The Seine built the city: its islands were defensible, its water was the highway. Starting your Paris days afloat is the perfect overture, because the historic banks of the Seine are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and you'll watch eight centuries of Paris scroll past in an hour.

📖Before You Go — The Story

Paris exists because of the Seine. The boat slides past the whole biography of the city: Notre-Dame and the Conciergerie for the Middle Ages, the Pont Neuf for the Renaissance, the Louvre, the Musée d'Orsay, Les Invalides, the Grand Palais, and of course the Eiffel Tower, where you set off.

Keep an eye out for the Pont Neuf — its name means "New Bridge," but it's actually Paris's oldest surviving bridge, finished in 1607, and the first one built without houses crowded on top of it. A 400-year-old naming joke.

This is the ideal jet-lag activity on arrival day: you're seated, outdoors, for one easy hour, with an audio guide that turns a boat ride into a history lesson. (Tip: for first-timers who don't know the skyline yet, the audio guide is the difference-maker.)

🧭On the Water

~20 min early
Find the right dock
Bateaux Parisiens leaves from Port de la Bourdonnais, at the foot of the Eiffel Tower (pontoon 3). Don't follow signs to the Bateaux-Mouches dock — that's a different company.
Onboard
Grab the top deck
Best views. June afternoon sun is strong — hats or the shaded side.
1 hour
Round trip past the icons
Eiffel Tower → Orsay → Notre-Dame → Louvre and back.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • Bridge-counting contest: keep score as you slip under each bridge.
  • The gold statues on the Pont Alexandre III — the fanciest bridge in Paris.
  • The "New Bridge" (Pont Neuf) that's really the oldest — groan together.
  • Wave at people on the riverbanks — Parisians genuinely wave back at the boats.
