Tower Bridge, Borough Market & the London Eye

Tue Jun 16 • Afternoon & evening
The Big Picture Two London themes meet this afternoon. Stone keeps the score: Victorian Tower Bridge (1894) is empire-era steel dressed in Gothic stone to flatter its medieval neighbor. And modern London is the remix: the South Bank, once the "naughty" theatre district, is now the culture mile, crowned by the year-2000 London Eye.

📖Before You Go — The Story

Tower Bridge (the famous one)

Opened in 1894 after eight years' work, with about 11,000 tons of steel — then disguised in stone so it wouldn't clash with the Tower of London next door. Its high walkways are 42 m above the river, and since 2014 they have glass floors: stand on nothing and watch buses cross beneath your feet. (Quick correction the kids will love: this is Tower Bridge — the plain one upstream is London Bridge, and yes, an old London Bridge really was sold and rebuilt in Arizona.)

Borough Market

There's been trading on this Southwark spot for about 1,000 years — the market is older than the Tower of London. Its lovely iron-and-glass halls date to 1851. But check the day: the full food market typically runs Wed–Sat, and Jun 16 is a Tuesday, so confirm opening before promising a feast.

The London Eye

135 m tall, opened in 2000, with 32 capsules — one for each London borough. It deliberately skips the number 13, so the capsules are numbered 1–33. It's not a thrill ride: it's one smooth, slow, 30-minute rotation, all about the view.
