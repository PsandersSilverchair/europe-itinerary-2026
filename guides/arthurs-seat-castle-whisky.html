Arthur's Seat, the Castle & Whisky

Fri Jun 12 • All day
The Big Picture Today the "rock writes the story" theme comes full circle. You'll climb an extinct volcano (Arthur's Seat), tour a fortress built on its volcanic twin (Castle Rock), and stand near where James Hutton — staring at this very rock — figured out that the Earth is unimaginably old and founded the science of geology. Fire, ice, fortress, and the birth of deep time, all in one day.

📖Before You Go — The Story

A volcano before breakfast

Arthur's Seat rises 250.5 m (822 ft) and is the eroded stump of a volcano whose lava is about 335–341 million years old — older than the dinosaurs by ~100 million years. On the way up, near Salisbury Crags, is "Hutton's Section," where James Hutton spotted molten rock that had forced its way through older layers and realized the planet had to be far older than anyone thought.

And a genuine unsolved mystery: in 1836, boys hunting rabbits on the hill found 17 tiny coffins, each holding a little carved figure. Eight survive in the National Museum of Scotland — and nobody knows for sure who made them or why (one theory ties them to the Burke & Hare murders).

The most besieged place in Britain

Edinburgh Castle has been attacked an estimated 26 times in 1,100 years. Inside sits the tiny St Margaret's Chapel (c. 1130), Edinburgh's oldest building, and the Honours of Scotland — the oldest crown jewels in the British Isles. They were locked in a chest after the 1707 union and forgotten for 111 years, until novelist Walter Scott had the room opened in 1818 and found them still wrapped in linen. Every day (except a few holidays) the One O'Clock Gun fires from the ramparts.

Whisky, kid-style

Next door, the Scotch Whisky Experience runs a barrel "dark ride" through how whisky is made, and shows off a collection of 3,384 bottles. Kids are welcome — they get a soft drink at the tasting.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • You climbed a real extinct volcano before lunch — older than the dinosaurs.
  • Mons Meg, a giant 1457 cannon that could fire a 150 kg stone — but oxen could haul her only ~3 miles a day.
  • The Crown Jewels that got lost inside their own castle for 111 years.
  • The One O'Clock Gun — cover your ears and watch the crowd flinch.
  • Myth-buster: the famous Stone of Destiny is no longer here — it moved to Perth Museum in 2024. The Honours (crown, sceptre, sword) are still here, though!
