Westminster, the Abbey & Covent Garden

Mon Jun 15 • Around the Big Bus route
The Big Picture This is the day to feel the trip's biggest London theme: the crown as a 1,000-year stage show. Westminster Abbey has crowned every English and British monarch since 1066, and the Changing of the Guard is the monarchy literally performing on schedule. Levi's royal-history quest peaks here — including one famous mystery to solve.

📖Before You Go — The Story

Guards who are real soldiers

The Changing of the Guard at Buckingham Palace happens on set days at 11 a.m. (Jun 15 is a Monday, so it should be on — but always check the morning of, as it can change). The bearskin-capped guards aren't costumed actors; they're working soldiers from the Foot Guards regiments.

The coronation church

Westminster Abbey has hosted coronations since 1066 — 40 monarchs, most recently Charles III in 2023 — and the Coronation Chair has been used since 1308. It's also a marble who's-who: Isaac Newton, Charles Darwin, and the poets of Poets' Corner. Elizabeth I and her rival half-sister "Bloody" Mary I share one tomb; Mary, Queen of Scots was reburied here by her son.

Levi's mystery to solve

Here's the big one: Queen Elizabeth II is NOT buried at Westminster Abbey. Her funeral service was held here in 2022 (that's the source of the confusion), but she rests at Windsor Castle, in the King George VI Memorial Chapel she had built herself, beside her parents and Prince Philip. Windsor is about an hour from central London if you decide a side-trip is worth it.

One bell, one nickname

"Big Ben" is really the nickname of the Great Bell (13.7 tonnes), not the tower. The tower was renamed the Elizabeth Tower in 2012 for Queen Elizabeth's Diamond Jubilee.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • Guards in tall bearskin hats — real soldiers, not actors. Can you make one smile? (You can't. They won't.)
  • In the Abbey, find the tomb where two rival queens (Elizabeth I & Mary I) lie together.
  • Detective challenge: Queen Elizabeth II is not buried here — she's at Windsor. Can you tell anyone who gets it wrong?
  • "Big Ben" is the bell, not the tower (that's the Elizabeth Tower).
  • A street performer in Covent Garden frozen like a living statue.
