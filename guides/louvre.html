The Louvre

Mon Jun 22 • 3:30 PM • Pyramid / Carrousel entrance
The Big Picture The Louvre is the trip's "who owns Paris?" theme in one building. It began around 1190 as a fortress, became a royal palace, and in 1793 — during the Revolution — opened as a public museum. The king's private art became everybody's art. (And yes: Louis XIV grew up here, before he built Versailles, which you saw on Saturday.)

📖Before You Go — The Story

Today it's the world's most-visited museum (8.7 million visitors in 2024), entered through I. M. Pei's glass Pyramid (1989). But the best secret is downstairs: in the Sully wing you can walk around the actual moat and towers of the 1190s fortress, hidden beneath the museum.

The most famous resident, the Mona Lisa, is smaller than everyone expects (77 × 53 cm). She became the world's most famous painting partly because she was stolen in 1911 by an Italian handyman who'd worked at the museum — he simply walked out with her under his smock. For two years, people lined up to stare at the empty hook on the wall.

The museum is vast, so the goal with kids is not "see everything" (impossible and miserable) — it's a short hit list of ~8–10 great works.

🧭On Tour — A Kid-Sized Hit List

Mostly the Denon wing + Sully. Aim for these and call it a triumph:

1
The Medieval Louvre moat
Sully, lower ground — the fortress under the museum.
2–3
Venus de Milo & Winged Victory
The Winged Victory crowns the grand staircase.
4–5
Mona Lisa & the giant Wedding at Cana
See her first (or circle back) — she's small and behind glass, and her room is a scrum.
6–10
Liberty Leading the People, Raft of the Medusa, the Great Sphinx of Tanis, the Code of Hammurabi, Napoleon III Apartments
Mix of drama, Egypt, and royal bling — stop when stamina runs out.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

  • The Mona Lisa was once carried out the door under a workman's smock — find her and see how small she is.
  • Walk around a real 800-year-old fortress moat hidden in the basement.
  • The giant Sphinx of Tanis in the Egyptian rooms.
  • The Code of Hammurabi — laws carved in stone ~3,800 years ago.
  • Royal link for Levi: kings lived here before Versailles.
