Edinburgh • Arrival Day Ideas

The Royal Mile & Calton Hill

Thu Jun 11 • Afternoon & evening
The Big Picture Everything you'll see in Scotland starts with fire, then ice. About 340 million years ago this was a field of volcanoes; two dead cores still dominate the city — Castle Rock and Arthur's Seat. Ice-age glaciers scraped past the hard rock and left a long sloping ridge behind it. That ridge is the Royal Mile: the whole medieval city grew along one street, running downhill from the fortress to the palace. You're sleeping right inside that story.

📖Before You Go — The Story

You'll land jet-lagged on Jun 11, and that's actually perfect: today is a gentle, no-agenda stroll through 900 years of history that happen to be on your doorstep. The "Royal Mile" isn't one street with a sign — it's a string of streets (Castlehill, the Lawnmarket, the High Street, the Canongate) running from Edinburgh Castle down to Holyrood. And it really is about a mile long — but a Scots mile, which is longer than a Virginia one (~1.81 km).

Because the old city was squeezed onto one defensible ridge and ringed by walls, it couldn't spread out — so it grew up into towering tenements and down into the steep alleys called "closes" that drop off the Mile on both sides. Your apartment lives on one of them (Campbell's Close). Rich and poor were stacked on top of each other, and the closes were once the smelliest, most crowded streets in Europe.

Halfway down stands St Giles' Cathedral, founded in 1124, with a famous 15th-century "crown" spire. This is where the firebrand preacher John Knox launched Scotland's Reformation. Legend says that in 1637 a market trader named Jenny Geddes hurled her stool at the dean for reading the king's new prayer book — sparking a riot that helped tip Britain into civil war. (Historians aren't sure Jenny was real, so you get to be the judge.)

End the day up on Calton Hill, a ten-minute walk away, where Edinburgh tried to build its own Greek Parthenon in 1826 — and ran out of money after just 12 columns. Locals nicknamed the half-finished ruin "Edinburgh's Disgrace." In mid-June this far north, the sky stays light until almost 10 p.m., so this is a brilliant after-dinner spot.

🧭On Tour — What to See & Do

Afternoon
Wander the Royal Mile
Step into St Giles' (free / donation). Hunt for the Thistle Chapel and its carving of an angel playing the bagpipes. Outside the west door, find the heart-shaped Heart of Midlothian mosaic — and spit on it like a local (it marks a demolished prison).
Afternoon
The magic is one step off the Mile
Most visitors march down the middle and miss the closes. You live in one — duck into a few and feel how the old city stacked itself.
5:00 PM
The Real Mary King's Close
Your booked underground tour — see its own guide page. Two minutes from St Giles'.
Evening
Calton Hill at golden hour
Easy 10-minute walk, short climb, huge reward: the whole city, Arthur's Seat, and out to the sea. Great after dinner thanks to the late northern light.

🕵️For Levi & Micah — Spot These!

You're history detectives today. Find each of these and you win:

  • The heart in the pavement outside St Giles' — people spit on it for luck. (It marks the door of a vanished prison.)
  • A national hero (John Knox) is buried under parking space number 23 beside the cathedral. Find the little marker.
  • The angel playing bagpipes carved inside the Thistle Chapel.
  • Scotland's unfinished Parthenon on Calton Hill — only 12 columns. Your verdict: disgrace or masterpiece?
  • "Gardyloo!" — in the old days people threw their waste out of high windows into the closes. Imagine the smell.
